# substack-notes

Draft and improve Substack notes using the template system from Alesia Zakharova's analysis of her 12 best-performing notes, published as "How I Used Claude to Crack the Code of Viral Substack Notes". Notes are the short posts in Substack's Notes feed, one of the biggest subscriber-growth channels. The author grew from 0 to 3,653 subscribers publishing only on Substack, largely through notes.

Source article (free methodology; the author also sells a fuller paid playbook that is NOT part of this skill):
https://alesiazakharova.substack.com/p/how-i-used-ai-to-crack-the-code-of-f44

## What Notes are

- Short-form posts in the Substack Notes feed (Substack caps a note at 600 characters).
- One of the biggest growth drivers: in the analysis, top notes converted 2-43 new subscribers each.
- Cadence matters: 1-2 notes per day for visibility.

## The three templates

### Template 1: Milestone Celebration + Gratitude (best performer)

Performance in the analysis: 40-706 likes, 2-43 new subscribers.

Structure:
1. Announce the milestone (subscriber count, time milestone, or achievement)
2. Share the numbers (posts written, notes published, current stats)
3. Express genuine gratitude to the community
4. Reflect on the journey (what it means beyond the numbers)

Example openings:
- "3 months on Substack! 🥳🎉😀"
- "44 Subscribers! 😃 I'm definitely not winning any speed races..."
- "A three weeks ago, I was daydreaming about hitting 50 subscribers..."

Key elements: vulnerability about progress, community over numbers, authentic celebration of small wins, personal reflection on growth.

### Template 2: Strategy Share + Actionable Tips

Performance: 46-76 likes, 3-5 new subscribers.

Structure:
1. Hook with a relatable question or observation
2. Share specific, actionable strategies (a bulleted list works well)
3. Include timeframes and results for credibility
4. End with encouraging advice or a key insight

Example openings:
- "A few people have asked me in the comments how I got my first 50 subscribers..."
- "What helped me jump from 44 to 96 subscribers:"
- "Wow, I thought it was a myth that after your first 100 subscribers, it's easier to grow..."

Key elements: specific daily/weekly actions, real numbers and timeframes, practical advice others can implement, personal experience as proof.

### Template 3: Community Connection + Engagement Driver

Performance: 43-143 likes, 3-43 new subscribers.

Structure:
1. Pose a thoughtful question or share a personal pivot/challenge
2. Share your own experience or perspective
3. Invite others to connect and participate
4. End with a clear call-to-action for engagement

Example openings:
- "Is anyone else here trying something completely new in mid-life?"
- "Dear Substackers, what do you think on using AI for writing?"
- "To help you get more visibility, please drop your link below..."

Key elements: direct questions to the community, personal vulnerability or life changes, a clear invitation for others to share, supportive and inclusive tone.

## Universal success factors

Apply these to every note regardless of template:

- **Tone:** authentic and conversational (like talking to friends), vulnerable about struggles and slow progress, generous with support for others, genuinely excited about small wins.
- **Engagement tactics:** use emojis strategically for emotion, include specific numbers for credibility, ask questions to drive comments, share both wins AND challenges, focus on community over self-promotion.
- **Timing and consistency:** 1-2 notes daily for visibility, celebrate milestones at any size, share the journey not just destinations, be patient with growth ("slow and steady").
- **Community-first:** actively support other creators, share others' content generously, prioritize genuine connections over metrics, emphasize belonging ("finding your tribe").

## Workflow

### Mode A: draft a note

When the user asks for a note or note ideas:

1. Ask what they want to share: a milestone, a strategy/tip, or a question/community moment. If they have a topic in mind, match it to the closest template. Don't force a template onto content that doesn't fit.
2. Collect real specifics before drafting: actual numbers, dates, milestones, the strategy itself. If any detail is missing, ask for it or leave a clearly marked blank like [YOUR MILESTONE].
3. Draft the note following the chosen template's structure and opening-line pattern.
4. Apply the universal success factors (emojis sparingly, real numbers, one question, wins and challenges, community-first).
5. Keep each note under 600 characters, conversational, with a line break or two for readability.
6. Offer 1-3 variations when the user wants options, varying template and tone.

Fill-in-the-blank shape (milestone example):

```
[NUMBER] [MILESTONE] on Substack! 🎉
[STATS LIST]
[HUMBLE ACKNOWLEDGMENT]
[GRATITUDE STATEMENT] ❤️
```

### Mode B: audit existing notes

When the user provides 10-15 past notes with metrics (likes, new subscribers):

1. Group them by theme and identify common patterns in openings, structure, and tone.
2. Report which patterns correlate with the best performance.
3. Extract 3 fill-in-the-blank templates personal to the user, following the blueprint: gather data, ask the right questions, create templates, test and iterate.
4. For underperforming notes, diagnose what likely went wrong (no question, no numbers, self-promotional tone, weak opening).
5. Suggest concrete next tests (a new opening style, a different template, cadence changes).

## Guardrails

- Templates are structure, not substance. Never invent milestones, subscriber counts, stats, or stories. If the user has not provided real details, ask for them or leave a labeled blank.
- Authenticity is the real predictor of performance: the source article itself warns that notes built only on templates still fail if they don't contain the writer's real stories and feelings. Always ground every note in something true about the user.
- Don't promise virality. Frame templates as raising the odds, not guaranteeing results.
- Only the free article's methodology is encoded here. The author's paid playbook is a separate product; if the user wants it, point them to the source article.
